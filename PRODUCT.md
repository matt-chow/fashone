# Fashone — product context

> Inferred from the owner's written brief (Nov 2026); the owner asked for autonomous work without interviews.

**What it is.** *Fashone — The Illustrated Handbook of Men's Style.* A static, local-first web app that teaches menswear through four connected experiences: the Handbook (a curriculum read like an illustrated book), the Encyclopedia (a searchable garment catalogue with anatomy diagrams), the Outfit Studio (a layered fashion-plate figure you dress), and the Style Atlas (styles and traditions as overlapping influences).

**Who it serves.** Complete beginners, men improving how they dress, enthusiasts of tailoring and history, and readers curious about menswear as culture. Beginner-approachable, with depth on demand.

**Philosophy.** Classic foundations, creative freedom. Explain principles, trade-offs and context; never one correct way to dress. Separate convention from interpretation. No arbitrary scores, no gamified streaks.

**Voice.** A knowledgeable, well-edited handbook: concise, specific, warm, lightly witty. No filler, no invented citations.

**Brand commitments (pinned).** Name "Fashone". A 1930s–50s illustrated menswear catalogue and tailoring-publication world made interactive. Four themes: The Tailor's Study (default: ivory, burgundy, forest, brass), The Heritage Catalogue (cream, leather, tan, sepia), The Midnight Edition (navy, parchment, gold, charcoal), The Modern Archive (olive, rust, warm paper, charcoal). Original procedurally drawn vector illustrations in a fashion-plate manner, joined by a small curated set of archival plates (public domain or openly licensed only; provenance in `src/data/archive.json`, shown at `/credits`), and openly licensed photographs of real garments and people (`src/data/photos.json`), shown untinted so the colours stay accurate.

**Constraints.** Zero cost; static hosting; no accounts or backends; accessible (WCAG AA), responsive, reduced-motion aware.

**Modes.** Handbook, Encyclopedia, Atlas: Read. Outfit Studio: Operate. Homepage: Persuade, but in the publication's own voice (a contents page, not a SaaS hero).
